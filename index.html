Research

I develop cognitive and computational models of human behavior in traffic, with a focus on how drivers make decisions under uncertainty when interacting with automated vehicles. My work bridges cognitive neuroscience, human factors, and AI, contributing to safer, more human-compatible autonomous driving systems.

I publish in intelligent transportation (Transportation Research Part F, IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems, Accident Analysis and Prevention), general science journals (Nature Communications, PNAS Nexus, Royal Society Open Science), and cognitive science & computer science venues (Computational Brain and Behavior, Human Factors, IJCAI, ECAI, AAMAS).

MSc Supervision

Since 2021, I have supervised over 30 MSc thesis projects across multiple TU Delft faculties (Mechanical Engineering, Robotics, Civil Engineering, EEMCS). Approximately half resulted in peer-reviewed publications, and several students went on to pursue PhDs. Projects span driver behavior modeling, meaningful human control, and human-robot interaction, as well as interdisciplinary topics including rehabilitation robotics and AI alignment. One project (Hagenus, 2024) was recognized as the Best AI-related TU Delft MSc thesis of the year.
